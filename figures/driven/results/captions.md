# Captions for the driven-study figures

These captions accompany the eight figures generated by
`visualization/driven/plot.py`. The baseline reproduction is a separate, frozen
record. Figures 1–5 form the main response narrative; Figures 6–8 document
preparation, finite-volume and numerical limits and may be placed in supporting
material when preparing a manuscript.

## Common definitions and reporting conventions

The model has open boundaries, $ag=m/g=1$ and $\hbar=1$. Dynamics start from
the exact neutral ground state $|0\rangle$ of $H_0$ and end at zero external
field. $H(\epsilon)=H_0+\epsilon V+c\epsilon^2I$; $\epsilon$ is dimensionless.
Unless specified, $N=8$. Define $P_j=1-|\langle0|\psi_j(t_f)\rangle|^2$,
$W_j=\langle H_0\rangle_j-E_0$, $\Delta P_{\rm exc}=P_{s+w}-P_s-P_w$,
and $\Delta W=W_{s+w}-W_s-W_w$. The subscripts identify separate strong-only,
weak-only and combined trajectories from the same vacuum.

These are excitation probabilities and absorbed energies, not pair counts
or decay rates. TDSE labels denote numerical time-dependent Schrödinger propagation; LR
denotes the leading weak-field response prediction. All probabilities are
unconditional. Lines joining sampled
points guide the eye unless explicitly identified as a response prediction.
Error bars, where available, are numerical refinement estimates, not
statistical confidence intervals; bars smaller than markers may be invisible.
The probability resolution floor is $10^{-8}$; this is an operational
threshold, not a rigorous floating-point error bound. Independent estimates
were not calculated for every derived control or spatial quantity; those
panels display values without invented error bars. Original numeric files
are retained, including unresolved values.

## Figure 1 — Accessible transitions and charge sectors

**(a)** Neutral excitation gaps versus static background. Gray points show
levels; colored points show weights
$B_n=|\langle n;\epsilon_b|V|0;\epsilon_b\rangle|^2/g^2>10^{-8}$, using a
shared logarithmic color scale and a shared linear marker-area mapping.
Weights for degenerate eigenspaces are summed. **(b)** Unrestricted levels
relative to the lowest neutral energy at the same background, colored by
integer charge $Q$. **(c)** Zero-background transition strengths on a
logarithmic scale; weights at or below the same threshold are omitted.
All 79 saved backgrounds are used. Panels (a,c) display gaps through $8g$;
panel (b) displays relative energies from $-2g$ to $8g$. These are display
windows, not Hilbert-space truncations. The dominant neutral transition has
$(E_1-E_0)/g=2.657994$ and $B_1=0.216568$. The lower unrestricted charged gap
is inaccessible to charge-conserving evolution from $Q=0$.

## Figure 2 — Weak-probe validation and approximation limits

A compact probe is $\epsilon(t)=\epsilon_w\sin(\omega t)
\sin^2(\pi t/T_w)$ on $[0,T_w]$. **(a)** Normalized excitation at
$\epsilon_w=0.01$, $gT_w=32,64$, compared with leading weak-field response
using the Fourier transform of the actual finite waveform and the complete
neutral spectral weights. **(b)** Amplitude scaling at $\omega/g=2.658$ for
$\epsilon_w=0.0025,0.005,0.01$; dashed quadratic guides are anchored at the
lowest amplitude. The quoted slopes are descriptive three-point log-log
fits. **(c)** Relative departure $P_{\rm exc}/P_{\rm LR}-1$ for both durations
and all three main-scan amplitudes 0.01, 0.02, 0.04. Ratios are displayed
only where both probabilities exceed $10^{-8}$; NaN gaps prevent joining
through unresolved points. The signed logarithmic axis is linear within
$\pm10^{-3}$. The scan validates a weak resonant regime while displaying
its departures; it does not provide a fitted linewidth.

## Figure 3 — Absolute excitation and two-pulse nonadditivity

Compact Sauter strong and weak pulses have coincident centers, $gT_s=10$,
$0.1\le g\tau\le2$ and $\epsilon_w=0.01,0.02,0.04$. **(a,b)** Absolute
excitation for $\epsilon_s=0.6,1$, respectively: solid lines show combined
pulses, dashed lines weak-only, and dotted references strong-only. Open
downward triangles mark unresolved weak-only values at the display floor;
the strong-only 0.6 reference is an unresolved limit, not its raw value.
**(c,d)** Signed additive-comparison differences in excitation and absorbed
energy per site. All 150 main-scan points are included and the differences
are positive, permitting logarithmic axes. Colors identify weak amplitude;
circles/solid lines identify $\epsilon_s=0.6$, squares/dashed lines identify
$\epsilon_s=1$. Error bars add the constituent step/tolerance refinement
estimates. Positive nonadditivity alone does not establish an advantage over
a slow equal-peak input.

## Figure 4 — Peak, budget, timing and polarity controls

The upper and lower rows use $\epsilon_s=0.6,1$, respectively, with
$gT_s=10$ and the fixed-amplitude reference $\epsilon_w=0.01$.
**(a,d)** Combined excitation versus a slow Sauter pulse of the same peak
$\epsilon_s+\epsilon_w$ and width $T_s$. Open downward markers indicate the
unresolved slow-pulse response at background 0.6. **(b,e)** Combined response
with fixed weak-field input integral $\int\epsilon_w(t)^2dt$, compared with
the fixed-amplitude reference. The integral is matched to $g\tau=1$ using
the implemented compact waveform. It is not absorbed work. **(c,f)** At
$g\tau=0.5$, response for delays $gt_d=-10,0,10$ and an opposite-polarity
weak pulse at zero delay. Only the four saved widths and these three delays
are represented. Matched controls reject a peak-height-only explanation of
the selected background-0.6 result; background 1 shows strong polarity
sensitivity and a larger slow equal-peak response.

## Figure 5 — Response expansion and complete final-state decomposition

Use $\epsilon_w=0.01$ and the same strong pulse as Figure 4.
**(a)** Measured increment $\delta P=P_{s+w}-P_s$ versus a weak-amplitude
expansion through second order about the full strong-pulse trajectory,
including the second-order interference term. This increment differs from
$\Delta P_{\rm exc}$ by $P_w$. **(b)** Polarity-even and polarity-odd
increments, $\delta P_{\rm even}=(P_++P_-)/2-P_s$ and
$\delta P_{\rm odd}=(P_+-P_-)/2$. Their sum is $P_+-P_s$.
The signed axis is linear within $\pm10^{-9}$.
**(c,d)** At $g\tau=0.5$, signed contributions
$\Delta p_n=p_n^{s+w}-p_n^s-p_n^w$ from every one of the 69 zero-field
neutral excited states, plotted at their excitation energies. Blue/orange
indicates positive/negative contributions. Every state is included in the
verified sum $\sum_{n>0}\Delta p_n=\Delta P_{\rm exc}$; near-zero contributions
can overlap visually. The shaded $\pm10^{-8}$ band is a reference scale,
not a per-state confidence interval; the axis is linear within this band.
State populations describe final redistribution, not unique transition
paths. The expansion is not uniformly accurate at all widths.

## Figure 6 — Ramped-background preparation and frequency dependence

A zero-field vacuum is ramped to $\epsilon_b=0.6$ or 1, held during a
$\epsilon_w=0.01$, $gT_w=32$ sine probe with buffers, then ramped back to zero.
**(a)** Ramp-up infidelity $1-F$ to the lowest neutral background eigenstate,
measured before the probe, for $gT_r=10,20$. **(b,c)** Signed excitation
nonadditivity at the six sampled frequencies
$\omega/g=1,1.5,2,2.5,2.658,3$, with numerical refinement estimates.
Connecting lines do not resolve intervening extrema or zero crossings.
The background-1 ramp prepares only roughly 1–1.4% ground-state overlap
probability, whereas background 0.6 is prepared with high fidelity.
Both positive and negative nonadditivity occur. The poorly prepared
background cannot be interpreted as stationary ground-state spectroscopy.

## Figure 7 — Fixed-protocol size and spatial dependence

The background-1 two-Sauter protocol uses $\epsilon_w=0.01$, $gT_s=10$,
$g\tau=0.2,0.5,1$ and $N=8,12,16$, with fixed lattice spacing and couplings.
**(a,b)** Excitation nonadditivity and energy nonadditivity per site at all
nine measured points, with constituent numerical refinement estimates.
**(c)** At $g\tau=0.5$, regional mean nonadditivity of the vacuum-subtracted
local occupation $o_l=q_l^2$, averaged for $10/g$ after the pulse. The central
four sites are compared with the first two and last two sites. Explicitly,
$\Delta\bar o_R$ averages
$\overline{\langle o_l\rangle}_{s+w}-\overline{\langle o_l\rangle}_s-
\overline{\langle o_l\rangle}_w+\langle o_l\rangle_0$ over $R$.
No separate spatial error estimate is available. The positive selected
response persists, but no infinite-volume convergence or continuum
extrapolation is established; background 0.6 was not tested in this size scan.

## Figure 8 — Numerical checks at the representative small-signal point

Parameters are $N=8$, $\epsilon_s=0.6$, $\epsilon_w=0.01$, $gT_s=10$,
$g\tau=0.5$, $gt_d=0$. **(a)** Absolute difference between physical energy
change and integrated external-source work, including the scalar field
energy. **(b)** Norm drift without renormalization. Logarithmic diagnostics
place zeros at a display floor of $10^{-17}$; this is not a measured nonzero
error. Charge conservation is imposed by the neutral basis and is not an
independent leakage check. **(c)** Exponential-midpoint versus adaptive
propagation: absolute change in $\Delta P_{\rm exc}$ and the sum of absolute
changes of its three constituent probabilities for $gh=0.02,0.01$.
**(d)** Change in nonadditivity when the relative Sauter tail threshold is
reduced from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5},10^{-6}$. The dotted reference is the
measured signal, approximately $1.972\times10^{-6}$. These observed numerical
changes support resolution of this point; they are not rigorous error
bounds for the whole scan.
